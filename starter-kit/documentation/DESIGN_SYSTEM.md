# Sales Page Design and Content Guidelines

## Goal
Visitors should understand **what this product is, what it does, how to use it, and what to do next** within seconds. Minimal means **clear and direct**, not necessarily muted or luxury. Product campaign colours (red/yellow/orange/etc.) may be bold but text must remain readable.

## Essential narrative
1. **Hero:** name the product and its main benefit in plain, concrete language; show a real product image.
2. **Problem/context:** briefly identify the everyday need, without exaggeration.
3. **How it works:** 2–4 steps with pictures, captions or short video.
4. **Benefits:** lead with outcomes; explain unfamiliar features in simple words.
5. **Offer and price:** use approved terms and accurate offer IDs.
6. **Proof:** include authentic customer feedback, demonstrations, specs or policies only when verified.
7. **Inquiry/order form:** clear CTA and minimal required fields.
8. **FAQ:** only for real customer objections or practical questions.

## Content rules
- Prefer short sentences and ordinary language suitable for a broad audience, including young readers.
- Explain jargon with a one-line example; avoid vague claims like “revolutionary” without proof.
- Demonstration media should explain the **actual** product; do not invent before/after results.
- No fabricated scarcity, guarantee, testimonials, prices or ratings.
- Explain what happens after form submission (an order is placed with the ERP; do not imply a payment was taken).

## UI rules
- Mobile-first, readable minimum tap targets, labeled fields, clear error/success states.
- Prominent CTA near top and after key sections; form can be reached without navigating away.
- Optimize images (WebP/AVIF where suitable), meaningful alt text, lazy-load below-fold media.
- Respect user reduced-motion settings; don't use autoplaying audio.
- Use colour tokens per product and verify contrast for text and controls.
- Keep optional sections truly optional; no empty placeholders.

## Suggested page patterns (content variants, not separate applications)
- **Product-first:** hero/gallery → benefits → offers → form.
- **Demo-first:** hero → simple visual how-to → benefits → offers → form.
- **Offer-first:** hero + genuine offer → proof → how it works → form.

## Sign-off checklist
Can a first-time visitor identify the product, its benefit, actual offer price, and next action without scrolling through confusing marketing copy?
